## Assistant <sup>11/5/2026, 4:49:53 PM</sup>

**I wouldn't add this generic to `olo_fix_madd`:** it wouldn't save anything on AMD and would likely cost more. The array you want to remove also mostly exists only in the RTL, not in the hardware. I changed nothing in the repo.

## Can DSP slices get a fixed coefficient from the bitstream?
- **AMD:** the DSP registers can't be preset from the bitstream, as the `DSP48E1` and `DSP48E2` models in Vivado have no init parameters. A constant on the input pins does work without any runtime write, and that's what `CoefWritable_g=false` already does.
- **Intel:** at least on Cyclone V, yes. The DSP primitive has a coefficient table (`coef_a_0` to `coef_a_7`). I only saw that in the Quartus simulation models and haven't tried whether Quartus infers it from generic RTL. Using it directly would need vendor-specific code, which doesn't fit this library.

## Would a generic in `olo_fix_madd` help?
- **Fixed coefficients:** no, there's nothing left to save. The constant on `InB_Data` already leaves no coefficient logic at all.
- **Writable coefficients with init values:** no. The register can't be preset inside the DSP, so each slice would need either its own load mux at reset or a B register outside the DSP. Today that logic is shared.

## What the array really costs
Synthesis merges most of the array, because each bit only ever has a few distinct values. Here are the results for 8 taps, 1 channel and 18-bit coefficients on a Zynq-7000, all with 8 DSPs:

| Variant | Coefficient data FFs | Total FFs | LUTs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writable, loads `CoefInit_g` (current) | 42 (not 144) | 96 | 10 |
| Writable, no init values (one shared write register) | 28 | 81 | 9 |
| Fixed (current) | 0 | 45 | 0 |

The no-init variant still has 28 FFs rather than 18. Probably some are duplicated for fanout, but I didn't check.

## Decision for you
The only way to get the direct wiring you describe is to drop `CoefInit_g` in writable mode. `Coef_WrData` would then go through one register to all slices, and the array, the `Written` flag and the reload at reset would all go away.

- **Gain:** noticeably simpler RTL and docs, plus 15 FFs and 1 LUT saved in this build.
- **Cost:** every power-up needs a full coefficient write before the filter works. Until then the coefficients are 0 in AMD hardware and `'U'` in simulation.

I'd keep the init values, because the hardware saving is small and the other FIR filters also support `CoefInit_g` in RAM mode. If you always write the coefficients at startup anyway, dropping them is a reasonable simplification. Which do you prefer?

## The bypass costs fabric registers
The DSP savings are real, but each bypassed tap moves its data registers out of the DSP into fabric. Results on a Zynq-7000 with 8 taps:

| Coefficients | Channels | DSPs | FFs | LUTs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none zero | 1 | 8 | 45 | 0 |
| 4 of 8 zero | 1 | 4 | 226 | 0 |
| 4 of 8 zero | 2 | 4 | 387 | 2 |
| all zero | 1 | 0 | 13 | 0 |

For 1 channel that's 4 DSPs saved for 181 extra FFs. This is usually a good trade for a halfband, since DSPs tend to be the scarcer resource. If you want to reduce the extra FFs, one option is an SRL-based delay line, which would have to give up the reset on those data registers (the first register of a bypassed tap and the output of its delay).
